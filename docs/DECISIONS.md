### ADR-010 Gratuities are obligatory and separate (Accepted)
- **Context:** Faris: gratuities are always separate, vary by vessel, are obligatory, minimum US$5 per person per night.
- **Decision:**
  - A per-vessel gratuity rate is stored as `gratuity_pp_night_minor` with currency, and a database check enforces at least US$5.

### ADR-011 Cruise total excludes flights and extras; check-in to check-out (Accepted)
- **Context:** Faris: remove flights from the cruise total, because tours are extra. A cruise runs from check-in to check-out. Airport transfers are not part of the package and are offered separately on tour pages.
- **Decision:**
  - The cruise total holds the fare (plus the separate gratuity line) only.
  - Flights, transfers and tours are separate tour/extra products with their own orders (DASH-PAY-014).
  - Vessel pages state "From check-in on {day 1} to check-out on {last day}; transfers not included" unless the package explicitly includes them, and link to `/tours-extras/transfers/` (DATA-017, DATA-018).

### ADR-012 Child pricing (Accepted)

  - The CruiseSearch "Children" stepper becomes an age entry per child.
- **Open (Q48):** which date the age is measured on, what the percentage is applied to, whether operators can override it, and cabin-sharing limits.

- **Context:** Q3.

### ADR-026 Nilecruz collects payments and keeps 10% commission (Accepted; PROPOSED-013 approved)
- **Context:** Q51 and Q18. Faris: money goes to the portal, which keeps 10% commission and later transfers the rest to the operator.
- **Decision:**
  - Nilecruz is the merchant of record, with one Stripe account and no Stripe Connect (DATA-035).
  - Commission is 10% of each verified cruise payment. It is kept as a setting, reversed in proportion to refunds, and never taken on gratuities (DATA-036).
  - Each verified payment creates an operator payable (payment less commission). Admins record payouts made outside the system, and operators see their own balance read-only (DATA-037, DATA-038).
  - This supersedes the 5% in both repos.
- **Open (Q54):** whether the 10% also applies to tour orders.

### ADR-027 Payment deadlines measured from the booking date (Accepted)
- **Context:** Q16/Q17. Faris confirmed full payment within a deadline counted from the booking date, set by departure period. This replaces the earlier idea of paying 45 days or three months before departure.

  | Departure period | Full payment due |
  |---|---|
  | 24 Dec – 7 Jan, inclusive | Within 3 days of booking |
  | Premium periods (Easter 21–29 Mar, configured for 2027) | Within 3 days of booking |
  | Other October–April | Within 7 days of booking |
  | May–September | Within 14 days of booking |

- **Decision:**
  - The deadline is never later than departure.
  - Unpaid reservations are cancelled and their cabins released automatically at the deadline. A reported payment awaiting verification goes to admin review instead (DATA-040).
  - Cruise and tour payments stay separate. Each cruise has its own cancellation policy, acknowledged before payment (DASH-PAY-001…014).
  - **My calls, logged:** days are Cairo calendar days, the season is set by the departure date, and the rules are unit-tested at every boundary (DATA-039).
- **Consequence:** because a "reported payment awaiting verification" still exists, manual payment reporting (for example a bank transfer) remains alongside Stripe, verified by an admin. Q55 confirms.

## Batch 1, 17 Oct 2026

### ADR-033 No automatic language redirect (Accepted)
- **Context:** next-intl can redirect visitors by browser language.
- **Decision:** `localeDetection: false`. A URL always shows the same language, and the language switch is explicit.
- **Consequence:** crawlers and shared links get predictable pages (supports SEO-007 in Batch 10).

### ADR-034 CSP without a nonce (Accepted; revisit in Batch 11)
- **Context:** a nonce-based CSP forces every page to render dynamically, defeating static generation and caching (PERF-010).
- **Decision:** a strict CSP (`default-src 'self'`, `object-src 'none'`, `frame-ancestors 'none'`, `base-uri 'self'`, `form-action 'self'`) that allows inline scripts. The only inline script we author is the 3-line theme bootstrap.
- **Consequence:** weaker XSS protection than nonces. The Batch 11 security review decides whether to move to hashes or nonces once the page set is known.

### ADR-035 No Japanese web fonts (Accepted; changes the Q15 answer)
- **Context:**
  - Self-hosting Noto Serif JP and Noto Sans JP through next/font added about 200 KB (gzipped) of render-blocking `@font-face` CSS to **every** page, including English.
  - On `/ja/` it downloaded 25 font files (722 KB).
  - Lighthouse mobile LCP was 4.1 s (EN) and 8.1 s (JA).
- **Decision:**
  - No Japanese web font is downloaded. The design-system stacks still name Noto Serif JP and Noto Sans JP, so devices that have them installed (Android) use them, and others use their own Japanese faces (Hiragino, Yu).
  - Only first-paint Latin files are preloaded.
  - PERF-006 is reworded to match.
- **Consequence:** render-blocking CSS fell from about 200 KB to about 5 KB, and Japanese renders in system Mincho/Gothic faces (checked in screenshots). If Faris wants exact Noto JP rendering everywhere, the alternative is a JA-only layout loading a subset font, at a measurable LCP cost.

### ADR-036 CSS inlined; Lighthouse LCP reported as a warning pending Q58 (Superseded by ADR-040)
- **Context:** after ADR-035, Lighthouse's default **simulated** mobile LCP was:

  | Page | Simulated LCP (median) |
  |---|---|
  | `/` | 2.0 s |
  | `/es/` | 2.7 s |
  | `/ja/` | 2.7 s |
  | `/terms/` | 2.8 s |

  Results are bimodal (about 2.0 s or about 2.8 s for the same page). The model counts the React runtime's script evaluation (×4 CPU) whenever it starts before the text paints.

  Real throttled runs (Lighthouse "devtools" throttling: slow 4G, 4× CPU) gave:
  - LCP of 0.8–2.1 s on every page;
  - CLS of 0;
  - total blocking time of 18–307 ms.
- **Decision:**
  - `experimental.inlineCss` removes all render-blocking stylesheet requests.
  - LHCI keeps every threshold, but LCP is set to `warn` instead of `error` until Faris chooses the gate method (Q58). CLS, TBT and accessibility remain errors.
- **Consequence:** the gap is visible in every CI run, not hidden.

### ADR-037 Shell differences from the design-system NavBar (Accepted; logged in DS-DIFFS.md)
- **Decision:**
  - The theme switch is a small text control beside the language switch (left), and inside the menu on phones. The design system has no theme control in NavBar.
  - The phone menu is a `<details>` element (works without JavaScript) and includes the language switch, which the design system hides under 760 px.
  - The sliding underline animation is deferred to the full NavBar port in Batch 2.

### ADR-038 Theme handling survives client re-mounts (Accepted)
- **Context:** screenshots showed the 500 page in Day when Night was saved. React's re-mount after an error rebuilt `<html>` without `data-theme`.
- **Decision:**
  - The head script owns `data-theme`.
  - A tiny `ThemeSync` component re-applies the saved theme on mount.
  - The no-JS rule uses `<noscript><style>` instead of an `<html>` class.
- **Consequence:** a regression test was added to the 500-page test.

### ADR-039 Draft legal pages stay in English (Accepted)
- **Decision:** `/terms/` and `/privacy/` exist only in English while in draft. Spanish and Japanese footers link to the English page, marked "In English" with `lang="en"`. `/es/terms/` and `/ja/terms/` return 404.
- **Consequence:** this is a temporary exception to "links stay within one language" (I18N-003). It ends when translated, approved texts exist.

### ADR-040 Mobile LCP gate uses Lighthouse's simulated throttling (Accepted)
- **Context:** Q58. Faris chose the strict, simulated Lighthouse test as the gate.
- **Decision:**
  - LHCI asserts LCP ≤ 2,500 ms as an **error** again (median of 3 mobile runs, simulated throttling).
  - CLS ≤ 0.05, TBT ≤ 200 ms and accessibility ≥ 0.95 also remain errors.
  - The CI run on GitHub's runner is the record of truth. Local runs on a busy developer machine are noisier, as Batch 1 showed.
- **Consequence:** every batch must keep client JavaScript lean. A failing LCP blocks the PR, as intended.

### ADR-041 The strict Lighthouse gate runs against Railway deployments (Accepted)
- **Context:** Q59. On localhost the simulated LCP flips between about 2.0 s and 2.8 s for the same page, because the trace it replays is unrealistically fast. Faris chose to run the strict gate on real deployments.
- **Decision:**
  - New workflow `.github/workflows/lighthouse-deployed.yml` triggers when Railway reports a successful deployment, for PR environments and staging. It can also be run by hand with a URL.
  - It checks `/api/health`, then runs LHCI with `lighthouserc.deployed.json`: simulated mobile throttling, LCP ≤ 2.5 s as an **error**, CLS, TBT and accessibility as errors.
  - The localhost run in `ci.yml` stays, with LCP as a **warning** only.
- **Setup it needs:**
  - Railway PR environments enabled.
  - A `RAILWAY_STAGING_URL` repository variable, used when Railway reports only its dashboard link.
- **Consequence:** the gate reflects a real network. A PR's performance result arrives after its Railway deployment, not with the first CI run.

### ADR-042 Railway service runs in EU West (Amsterdam) (Accepted)
- **Context:** in its first region, the staging service took about 230–250 ms to send the first byte even for cached, prerendered pages. Requests came through Railway's Paris edge (`cdg1`) and a US runner. That alone pushed `/es/` 5 ms over the strict LCP budget (2,505 ms).
- **Decision:** Faris moved the service to EU West (Amsterdam) on 17 Oct 2026. Server time from here fell to about 70 ms; from GitHub's runner it was 100–200 ms.
- **Consequence:**
  - The strict gate passes on all four pages. The thinnest margin is `/terms/` at 2.42 s in one run.
  - Pages are served by `next start` with no CDN in front. Before launch (Batch 11), a CDN for static HTML should be evaluated for visitors far from Europe (Japan, the Americas).

### ADR-068 Account security details (Accepted)

- **Password from a reset link:** setting a password without the current one is allowed only in a session that came from a reset link within the last hour; an ordinary (or stolen) session can't.

- **No account probing:** sign-up, reset and resend answer the same way whether or not an account exists; a wrong password and an unknown email give the same message.

### ADR-085 API requests are signed with an Ed25519 key pair, not a shared secret (Accepted; Faris 18 Oct 2026)
- **Context:** the 4c brief asked for HMAC-signed calls with "only a hash of the secret stored". The two can't both hold: checking an HMAC needs the secret itself, so a stored hash would either be useless or would effectively be the secret.
- **Decision:** the operator's key is an Ed25519 key pair made when they click "Create a key". The private key (PEM) is shown once and never stored; Nilecruz keeps the public key (32 bytes) and a public key ID (`nck_…`). Each request signs `nilecruz-v1`, method, path, timestamp, idempotency key and the body's SHA-256; requests more than five minutes from our clock are refused; the idempotency key (kept 24 hours per company) returns the first answer to any repeat. Two keys can be active at once for rotation (SEC-008). Outbound webhooks (4e) are signed the same way with a Nilecruz key. The scheduler's internal pull call, where both sides are ours, uses HMAC with a shared secret.
- **Consequence:** a copy of our database can't be used to call the API as an operator. Operators need an Ed25519 library (standard in Node, Python, PHP sodium, Java 15+, .NET 8 via libraries); the format document has a working Node example.

### ADR-090 Payments and tours: Faris's answers on 19 Oct 2026 (Accepted; Batch 4f-2)
- **Stripe:** the sandbox account is registered in Spain, with euros by default, and can charge E£, US$ and €. Test keys are in staging and `.env.local`. Live keys come only with launch.

### ADR-092 Travel partners (Accepted; Faris 20 Oct 2026, Batch 4h brief)
- **Rates:** partners see their company rate first, then the trade rate, then the public international rate, labelled "public rate". Trade and company rates are never visible to customers, the public, other companies or other operators.
- **Commission:** the same 10 % of each payment on partner bookings, with the rest payable to the operator.
- **Client booking record:** the agency's name and selling price; "Booked through Nilecruz" in small print only; never the trade price.
- **Applying:** company name, country, registration or licence number, website, and a contact name and phone (brief question 1, as recommended).
- **Paying:** partners pay like customers: the same deadlines, card or transfer, to Nilecruz. Credit terms are out of scope (brief question 4, as recommended).
- **Two pull requests:** 4h-1 (applying, approval and pausing, team roles, trade and company rates, search) and 4h-2 (reserving, company requests and offers, bookings and payments, client references, selling totals, client record).
